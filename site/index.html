Any button is now a controller.

Orbit turns your earbuds, a keyboard knob, any key, mouse buttons or trackpad gestures into a wheel of tools for your computer and phone. Scroll reels, flip slides, scrub video, switch apps.

Volumemove to pick · press to choose

Three moves. That's the whole thing.

No new hardware. Orbit listens to buttons you already have: earbuds, a keyboard knob, a key, a mouse button, a trackpad gesture, or on Android the phone's volume keys and a tap on its back. Setup asks what you have.

Open

Press your "open menu" button (a double press on earbuds) and the wheel appears, wherever you are.

Move

Next / Previous (volume up / down, a knob, a swipe) glides to a tool; it lights up the wheel around it.

Select

Now your buttons drive that tool, until you open the wheel again.

What it can do

Pick what's on the wheel and its order in Settings, and which button does what in Settings → Controls.

Scroll & reelsSmooth page scrolling; one reel or Short per step on your phone.
SlidesOne slide per step. Press to black out the screen.
Video timelineGlide through DaVinci Resolve, Premiere or Final Cut. Press to play.
App switcherTurn through your open apps, let go to jump.
Volume & brightnessExact steps when slow, big moves when fast. Laptop screens too.
CameraTake a photo or start / stop a video from across the room. Great with a tripod.
TeleprompterAndroid: move the script, press to play or pause. Switch it on in the app's Wheel items.
Undo, tabs, desktopsStep back through history, tabs or virtual desktops.
Your own shortcutsAdd any key combo to the wheel.

Download

Install Orbit and it asks what you want to control it with: earbuds, a volume knob or media keys, mouse buttons, a trackpad, the phone's volume keys or back tap, or set it up yourself. Change it any time in Settings → Controls. Full guide: README.

Windows

Windows 10 or 11. If Windows says "protected your PC", choose More info → Run anyway. Orbit lives in the system tray.

Download for Windows

macOS

macOS 13+ on Apple Silicon. Unzip, move to Applications, right-click → Open the first time, then allow Accessibility. After updating, switch Orbit off and on again in that list.

Download for Mac

Android

Android 8+. Install the APK and follow the setup in the app. If a switch is greyed out: App info → ⋮ → Allow restricted settings. Phone buttons work while the screen is on.

Download for Android

Every version and its notes: all releases. Want to know about updates? Click Watch → Custom → Releases on GitHub.

Questions

The wheel never opens.

Open Settings → Controls and press your "Open menu" button there: if it doesn't light up, Orbit isn't receiving it. Earbuds must be connected to this device, with their gestures set to play / pause, next and previous track. CMF Buds Pro 2: in Nothing X → Controls → Dial set single = Play/Pause, double = Next track, triple = Previous track, hold = Voice assistant. On Android, Help → "Wheel won't open?" turns green when a press arrives.

How do I turn Orbit off?

Windows and Mac: tray / menu-bar icon → untick "Orbit on". Android: the switch at the top of the app, "Turn off" on its notification, or the Orbit Quick Settings tile. While off, every button works as if Orbit weren't installed.

What can I control it with?

Any earbuds or headphones with buttons work (press, double, triple), and so do keyboard volume knobs, media keys, any key or shortcut, mouse side buttons, Ctrl + scroll and trackpad gestures, plus the phone's volume keys, remotes, selfie sticks, back tap, chop and twist on Android. Setup asks what you have; Settings → Controls changes any of it.

Trackpad gestures also switch my desktops (Windows).

Windows runs its own action for 3- and 4-finger gestures too. To use them only for Orbit, set them to "Nothing" in Settings → Bluetooth & devices → Touchpad.

Is it safe? What does it access?

Orbit runs only on your device and sends nothing anywhere (it doesn't even have internet access). The code is on GitHub. It sees button presses so it can match your triggers; presses no trigger uses go straight on and aren't kept. On Android it also reads the name of the app in front, the camera's buttons when you use the Camera tool, and the motion sensor only while you use back tap, chop or twist.

Why does Windows warn about the installer?

The installer isn't code-signed yet (that costs money every year), so Windows SmartScreen shows a warning for new apps. Choose More info → Run anyway. The source is public if you'd rather build it yourself.

Can I use the code?

Yes, free to use and change. If you share something built on Orbit publicly (an app, a fork, a video), credit "Based on Orbit by Yemcube" with a link to @yem.cube on Instagram. Full license.

Something isn't working, or I have an idea.

Ask in Orbit on Telegram, or open an issue with your device and the log (Windows: tray icon → Open log; Mac: ~/.orbit/orbit.log; Android: "Copy log" in the app).
